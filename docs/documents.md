# Documents

**Documents** (under **Others** in the menu) is the shop's filing cabinet: a
place to keep a photo or PDF of any paper, and find it again later.

Typical things to keep: vendors' paper bills, expense receipts, bank
statements, GST papers, staff papers such as payslips.

It only keeps files. It does not read them, and it does not make payslips or
work out salaries.

## Adding files

1. Open **Documents**.
2. Choose the folder the files belong in.
3. Press **Upload files** and pick one or more photos or PDFs (up to 20 at a
   time).

- Photos (JPG, PNG) and PDF files are accepted, up to 4 MB each. Other kinds of
  file are refused.
- A big phone photo is made smaller before it is sent, so it uploads quickly
  and takes little room. The writing stays readable.
- The file's name becomes the document's name. It can be changed afterwards.

## Folders

Purchase bills, Expense receipts, Bank, GST and tax, Staff, Other.

## Attaching a file to a bill or an expense

Open the bill or the expense and press **Attach a photo or PDF** under
**Attached files**. The file is kept in Documents and also shows on that bill
or expense, so the paper is one click away from the entry.

## Finding, opening and changing

- Search by name or by words in the notes, and filter by folder.
- Open a document to see it, **Open** it in a new tab or **Download** it.
- Its name, folder, the date on the paper and notes can be changed at any time.

## Who can see what

- Every signed-in user can see and add documents.
- The owner can tick **Only the owner can see this** on a document (for example
  bank papers and payslips). Staff then cannot find it or open it.
- Only the owner can delete a document. Deleting is for good.
- Files are private. There is no public link to a file; it is sent only to a
  signed-in user of this app.

## Limits

- All files together can take up to 300 MB. The Documents page shows how much
  is used. When it is full, delete files that are no longer needed.
- The **backup download** holds the list of documents (names, folders, notes)
  but not the files themselves. Keep the paper, or a copy of important files,
  somewhere else too.
